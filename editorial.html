Editorial standards

How KeepProof researches, writes and corrects its consumer education guides.

Our method

Each article starts with a specific reader problem: a missing receipt, an unclear warranty clause, a damaged parcel or a return deadline. We structure the answer around the facts a reader can verify, the evidence worth preserving, and a realistic next step. We favor plain-language explanations and hypothetical examples over unsourced confidence.

Primary sources and limits

We reference the US Federal Trade Commission and USAGov where relevant, and encourage checking the actual retailer or manufacturer policy. Sources in the directory are informational, not proof that a single rule applies worldwide. Laws, contracts and local consumer protections differ. We label representative examples clearly and do not imply real first-hand testing.

No invented urgency

We do not publish a daily policy feed without verification. Policy Watch articles explain how to check changing terms and identify original sources. They are not real-time databases of merchant terms.

Maintenance and corrections

Pages were first prepared on 11 November 2026. We will update pages when specific information is checked or materially revised, not merely to make them appear fresh. To flag an error, email raflipanel2@gmail.com with a source and the relevant page URL.

Tools and transparency

The Return Window Calendar performs date arithmetic only; it does not interpret retailer policies. Purchase Register uses local browser storage. Support Packet Builder composes a text draft in the current browser tab. The tools do not ask for receipts to be uploaded to KeepProof servers.

KEEP THE QUESTIONS CLEAR

For product-specific decisions, use the official seller policy and the actual contract. We are an independent education site.
