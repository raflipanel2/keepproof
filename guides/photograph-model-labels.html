FIELD GUIDE 04 / RECORDS

Photograph model and serial labels safely

A practical reference system for exact product identification.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

One product family can contain several versions with nearly identical names. The precise model, capacity or production revision may decide which manual, spare part or warranty applies. A label photo can prevent mistakes, but serial numbers can also be sensitive identifiers that should not be shared indiscriminately.

01Find the label before something breaks

Check the underside, rear panel, inside a battery cover, or the original carton. For large appliances, use the manufacturer manual to locate the label instead of moving heavy or wired equipment unsafely.

Photograph the complete label and the surrounding context. If a unit has multiple identifiers, note which one is the model and which one is the serial number. Do not confuse the marketing name with the manufacturer part number.

02Capture condition without disassembly

For expensive goods, record a simple exterior condition photo at delivery. A photograph is not proof that internal components worked, but it can establish obvious shipping damage or included accessories. Never open energized equipment merely to find an identifier.

If you are documenting an issue, keep separate before/after photographs with dates and do not modify them. A short written description of the symptom helps a support agent interpret an otherwise ambiguous photo.

03Control who sees identifiers

A serial number may help a manufacturer verify eligibility, but posting it publicly can create privacy and misuse risks. Keep the unredacted version in private storage. Share only the fields requested by an official support channel.

If you sell or give away a device later, remove personal accounts and data using the manufacturer’s instructions. Ownership transfer and warranty transfer are separate questions; confirm the latter in the actual terms.

04Connect images to purchase evidence

Name the label photo with the same product identifier as the receipt. Include one small text note containing the store name, purchase date, and where the warranty terms are stored. This “evidence bundle” can then travel with a claim without reconstructing the history.

If the label becomes inaccessible after installation, make the photo before installation, especially for fitted appliances and large fixtures. Safety and installation instructions always take priority.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

Two air purifiers look alike, but one has a different filter cartridge. A photo of the model plate resolves the difference without guessing from the product’s color. The photo is kept private and used only when ordering compatible parts or contacting support.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Photograph full model details in good light.
  2. Store originals privately; redact public copies.
  3. Connect the image to the receipt and warranty.
  4. Do not dismantle live equipment to reach a label.
