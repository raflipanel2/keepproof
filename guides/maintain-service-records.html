FIELD GUIDE 15 / OWNERSHIP

Keep a service history for appliances and electronics

Record repairs, parts and maintenance in a form the next owner can read.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

A service record is not just a warranty attachment. It helps you determine what was repaired, whether a symptom is repeating and which accessories or parts are compatible. A small, consistent log is better than a pile of disconnected invoices.

01Choose a clear event format

Write the date, product and model, symptom or maintenance reason, provider, work performed, parts replaced, and any amount paid. Add a support case ID or service invoice number. If you do not know the technical cause, describe what the provider reported instead of guessing.

Separate routine maintenance from repair after a failure. That helps an owner see whether the item was maintained or repeatedly malfunctioned.

02Preserve the service invoice

The receipt shows the provider and price; the work order may identify actual parts and labor. Save both when useful. A statement such as “fixed” may be insufficient if the same issue returns six months later.

Ask for written coverage on replaced parts and labor. A repair business may offer a separate service warranty that differs from the product manufacturer’s warranty.

03Avoid unsafe DIY assumptions

Do not treat a general educational checklist as a repair manual for electrical, gas or other hazardous systems. Use qualified services when appropriate and follow manufacturer maintenance instructions.

If you perform ordinary owner-approved maintenance, record the relevant material or filter model, but do not claim professional inspection that did not occur.

04Use records when selling or transferring

A concise, redacted service history helps a new owner understand a product’s condition. Remove billing addresses and private contact information before sharing. Warranty transfer rules may require additional steps.

Keep your full originals privately. The shared version should include relevant dates, work and product identifiers, not unnecessary personal details.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

An air conditioner has two service visits a year apart. The second technician sees that a drain component was replaced previously and can consider whether the new issue is related, rather than starting with no history.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Log dates, symptoms, work and provider.
  2. Attach full repair invoices privately.
  3. Record parts/labor coverage separately.
  4. Share only redacted service history.
