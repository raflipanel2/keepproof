FIELD GUIDE 02 / RECORDS

Save digital receipts without losing the context

What to preserve from email confirmations, apps and marketplace order pages.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

An email saying “your order is confirmed” may omit the item details or final payment amount. A good digital receipt capture preserves a complete transaction story: what was ordered, which seller supplied it, when it was paid for, and which terms were shown. Store context now so a claim does not depend on an app still showing last year’s order.

01Know which screen proves what

The checkout confirmation proves an order was submitted. A charged invoice shows a payment or bill. The shipment email shows movement; delivery confirmation shows when the parcel arrived. These records serve different purposes and may matter under different return policies.

When marketplace sellers and platforms are different businesses, record the actual seller as well as the platform. Customer service may route a warranty or return to the merchant who fulfilled the order rather than the platform you visited.

02Export instead of screenshotting everything

Prefer a downloadable invoice or PDF with the full item description, price, seller, order number and date. If an app has no export option, use Print to PDF from the page and check its final pages. A screenshot is a useful fallback, but it can crop a footnote or policy link.

Keep the relevant order email as a separate file if it records a promise, delivery deadline or support conversation absent from the invoice. Never add your account password or a one-time verification code to a stored receipt.

03Preserve important terms

If an expensive item includes a limited-time return program or promotional coverage, save the terms offered at checkout. The product description and the warranty booklet may not describe the same promise. Label the date you captured each document.

For subscriptions and digital licenses, the renewal rules and account history may be more useful than a physical-product model number. Check whether the purchase is tied to an account that can be accessed after changing email addresses.

04Design a simple retrieval test

Try finding a purchase using only its product name, not the merchant email subject line. If your search fails, improve the file name. Once per quarter, open a random saved PDF and check that attachments are not corrupted or password-locked.

Retain sensitive billing records only where needed, and restrict shared-folder permissions. Store a redacted copy when another household member needs a warranty receipt but not the complete billing information.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A wireless speaker bought through a marketplace appears in the order-history app, but the seller name is hidden in a submenu. Saving the invoice and seller details now lets the owner contact the correct party if the speaker develops a covered fault after the app changes its layout.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Save the final invoice as PDF.
  2. Capture seller and order reference.
  3. Archive any unusual return or warranty promise.
  4. Test retrieval using the product name.
