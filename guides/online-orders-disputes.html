FIELD GUIDE 13 / RETURNS

Create a clear timeline for an unresolved online order

Separate late delivery, missing goods and product defects.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

A late parcel, missing delivery and defective product are different problems. Each has its own timeline and evidence trail. Presenting them as one vague complaint often creates confusion, especially if the marketplace, seller and carrier are different organizations.

01Label the issue accurately

For a late shipment, record the promised dispatch and delivery dates. For a “delivered” parcel that is missing, keep the carrier scan, delivery photo and steps taken to check the location. For a defective item, focus on the product symptom and the actual return or warranty terms.

Do not accuse a driver or merchant of theft without evidence. A tracking scan may be mistaken or incomplete, and some deliveries are left in shared locations.

02Collect records in order

Build a chronological list: order placed, payment completed, promised delivery, shipping updates, arrival or non-arrival, first support contact, and responses. Link each milestone to a saved email or screenshot.

If a seller provides a new delivery promise, record it separately from the original. That helps clarify whether a customer-service commitment was met.

03Know which policy applies

Merchant terms, shipping guarantees, payment-provider dispute rules and consumer law are not interchangeable. Some payment disputes have short deadlines; check the official provider instructions rather than relying on generic online timelines.

The FTC publishes advice for online shopping and resolving problems with a business in the United States. Readers in other regions should check their relevant consumer-protection agency.

04Escalate with one concise summary

Write a factual message stating the problem, order number, what has already been attempted, and the outcome requested. Avoid threats or exaggerated claims. Ask for a support case reference and response time.

Keep original records and duplicates of documents you submit. If you seek help from a payment provider or consumer agency, provide the same organized timeline to avoid contradictory versions of events.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A package is marked delivered on November 8, but the building mailroom has no record. The buyer checks the carrier delivery photo and address, then asks the seller for next steps. A short timeline avoids confusing a missing-package report with a standard product return.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Identify whether issue is late, missing or defective.
  2. Build a date-ordered evidence timeline.
  3. Review seller and payment provider rules separately.
  4. Use official escalation channels.
