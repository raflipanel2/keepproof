FIELD GUIDE 12 / RETURNS

Document an item that arrives damaged

An evidence sequence from package delivery to first contact.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

A cracked product or dented carton can trigger different processes: retailer return, carrier damage claim, or manufacturer support. Acting quickly and preserving the physical evidence makes it easier for the responsible party to assess what happened.

01Record the delivery before disposal

Photograph the outer carton, shipping label, cushioning, product and damaged area. Take both wide and close views so the relationship between the packaging and item is clear. Preserve the carton and materials until the seller or carrier advises otherwise.

Do not stage damage or alter the item to make the defect more visible. If damage involves exposed wires, leaking chemicals, sharp glass or another hazard, prioritize safety and follow appropriate local guidance.

02Determine the right contact

Review the retailer delivery-damage instructions first; they may provide a defined window and return label. Keep a timestamp of when you reported the problem, especially when an item was delivered to a parcel locker or neighbor.

Some sellers require contacting the carrier; others manage shipping claims internally. Avoid starting several conflicting claim processes without understanding who is responsible.

03Describe requested resolution

State the model, order reference, delivery date, visible problem and whether the product was used. Ask whether they will replace, refund, repair or inspect it, and request instructions for retaining or returning the damaged goods.

If a video helps illustrate a mechanical failure, record it only when operation is safe. It is acceptable to say you have not tested a damaged electrical product for safety reasons.

04Keep a clear chain of events

Save delivery notifications, photos, support case numbers, offered remedy, shipping labels, dispatch receipts and final confirmation. This is useful if a carrier investigates or a replacement develops a separate problem.

When replacement arrives, note whether the warranty or return window applies to the original purchase or a new delivery date. Policies and consumer-law requirements differ.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A ceramic lamp arrives with a broken base. The buyer photographs the box and protective inserts before throwing anything away, then sends the retailer the delivery date and four photos. The seller can evaluate a replacement request without asking for a recreated unboxing.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Keep packaging until instructed otherwise.
  2. Photograph label, box, filler and damage.
  3. Contact the seller through the stated channel.
  4. Retain the case number and final disposition.
