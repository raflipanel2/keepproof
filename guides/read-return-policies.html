FIELD GUIDE 10 / RETURNS

Read a return policy as a timeline

Find the real start date, exceptions and refund conditions.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

A return policy with “30 days” in the headline can contain several separate clocks. One may start on purchase, another on delivery, and a holiday extension may apply to selected categories only. The important work is to identify the relevant rule before you calculate a date.

01Locate the start event

Read whether the policy counts from checkout, shipment, receipt, delivery or activation. Record the exact start date and what evidence supports it. A platform delivery timestamp might matter more than the order email for a policy based on receipt of goods.

Distinguish calendar days from business days. Check if the final day requires the carrier to receive a parcel, the retailer to receive it, or merely authorization to be requested.

02Map product-specific exceptions

Different rules may apply to electronics, opened consumables, custom goods, hygiene items, clearance purchases or digital products. Do not assume a general policy governs every item in the cart.

Look for condition requirements such as tags, original accessories, packaging or proof of purchase. If the rules are ambiguous, ask support for written clarification while you are still within the stated window.

03Calculate conservatively

Use the Return Window Calendar as a planning aid. It adds a number of calendar days to a start date, but it cannot infer exceptions, shipping transit time, local laws or a retailer’s business-day definition. Give yourself a buffer.

If a deadline falls on a holiday or weekend, do not assume it extends automatically. Read the policy or ask the retailer. Capture the terms with a date so you can show which version you consulted.

04Keep return evidence

Save authorization emails, tracking receipts, postal acceptance scans, and refund confirmations. Keep the original purchase evidence even after an exchange, because a replacement item may have separate coverage terms.

If a return is refused, ask for the reason in writing and match it to the exact policy clause. This gives you a clear factual basis for a subsequent conversation.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A product ordered November 1 arrives November 5. A policy that starts on delivery produces a different planning date from one that starts on purchase. KeepProof shows the date arithmetic, but the retailer’s actual terms decide which result matters.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Record the start event and clock type.
  2. Check item-category exclusions.
  3. Save the policy version and authorization.
  4. Start the process before the final day.
