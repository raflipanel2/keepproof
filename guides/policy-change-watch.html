FIELD GUIDE 18 / POLICY WATCH

Policy Watch: how to verify the return terms that apply to your order

A method for assessing changing retail policies without trusting old summaries.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

Return policies may change by season, promotion, product category and geographic market. Rather than publishing a list of supposedly universal deadlines that will quickly become stale, KeepProof shows how to establish the right terms for one actual order and archive the evidence you used.

01Start with the merchant’s current policy

Locate the returns page from the official retailer website. Check the country and store channel because an online purchase might follow different rules from a physical-store purchase. Look for a date showing when the policy was updated, though not every merchant provides one.

Read exceptions for sealed items, clearance, personalized goods and products with a separate manufacturer process. Promotional return windows may apply only to orders placed between specific dates.

02Reconstruct the terms at checkout

Review the order email, receipt, product listing and any PDF you saved when purchasing. A current web page may not describe the terms previously shown to you. Where the documents conflict, ask the merchant for a written explanation.

A web archive or screenshot can add context, but its existence does not settle every legal question. Consumer rights also depend on the applicable region.

03Use a simple change log

Record the merchant, policy-page URL, date checked, product category, start event, duration and any stated exceptions. Save a copy to the purchase folder. This is more useful than saving a screenshot with no notes about which order it relates to.

Update your return calendar only when you understand what changed. A headline “extended returns” does not mean every category is covered.

04Verify before publishing or sharing

If you maintain a blog or team knowledge base, label observations with the check date. Do not claim a general policy is current if you have not checked the official page recently.

This page presents a verification workflow, not a live database of retail return periods. The FTC’s consumer guidance provides a stable baseline: inspect store policies and keep purchase records.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A shopper sees a social post claiming a retailer has 90-day returns. The official policy reveals a 30-day standard window and a longer seasonal exception for selected dates. Recording the order date and category is more reliable than relying on the viral claim.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Check the official policy for your country and category.
  2. Compare current terms with purchase records.
  3. Save a dated version with the order.
  4. Never treat a social-media summary as policy.
