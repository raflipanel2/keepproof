FIELD GUIDE 09 / WARRANTIES

Repair, replacement or refund: what the terms actually say

Learn the common remedies without assuming one outcome is guaranteed.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

When a product fails, it is natural to ask for a refund immediately. But a warranty may provide for repair first, replacement under certain conditions, or another remedy. Separate your preferred outcome from what the contract and local consumer law actually require.

01Identify the promised remedy

Find the section titled remedies, warranty service or what the company will do. Determine whether the warrantor chooses the remedy or whether the purchaser has an option. Check if replacement units may be refurbished and how shipping and labor are handled.

US rules distinguish types of written warranties; other countries use different consumer guarantee systems. A generic online checklist cannot decide your legal remedy, so verify the applicable law for your location.

02Describe failure in operational terms

A precise description makes a remedy request easier to evaluate. Say what function no longer works, under what ordinary conditions, and what safe steps you already tried. Do not exaggerate the fault or imply manufacturer confirmation that has not happened.

If the product poses an immediate hazard, stop using it and follow official safety guidance. A claim process should never require continuing unsafe operation to collect evidence.

03Track each service attempt

Record authorization number, shipping date, service date, work performed, and whether the symptom returned. These notes help support staff distinguish a repeated problem from a new unrelated issue.

Save written statements about renewed or remaining coverage after repair. Replacement or repair does not always reset the original warranty term; examine the actual terms before relying on a new end date.

04Escalate with records, not assumptions

If repeated service does not resolve the problem, provide a concise timeline and ask what the written policy offers next. You can seek consumer-agency advice where appropriate.

Do not promise a chargeback or legal remedy will succeed. Payment disputes, retailer returns and warranty rights are different mechanisms with different time limits and evidence needs.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A desk lamp is repaired under warranty and then develops the same issue a month later. The owner can provide two dated service records and a concise symptom comparison. That creates a clearer basis for asking the warrantor what remedy comes next.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Identify available remedies in the contract.
  2. Keep the timeline of each service attempt.
  3. Request coverage details after replacement.
  4. Use official consumer resources for unresolved cases.
