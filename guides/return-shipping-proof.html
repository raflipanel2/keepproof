FIELD GUIDE 20 / RETURNS

Keep return-shipping proof until the refund is complete

Which postal records matter when a returned parcel disappears or arrives late.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

Sending a return parcel is not always the end of a return. Retailers may wait for a carrier scan, receipt at a processing center or inspection before issuing a refund. Track the chain of events and save enough evidence to ask a precise question if a parcel is delayed.

01Confirm authorization before dispatch

Check whether the merchant requires a return merchandise authorization, a specific label or a particular carrier. Confirm that the authorization is linked to the correct order and item. Some returns must be initiated within one window and physically delivered within another.

Read any special packaging instructions. For electronics, follow official battery-shipping guidance and carrier rules; general packing tips are not a substitute for transport requirements.

02Capture evidence at handoff

Save the shipping label and photograph the packed carton before closing it if the contents and condition matter. Obtain an acceptance or drop-off receipt where possible. A label created online proves preparation, not necessarily physical acceptance by the carrier.

Record the tracking number and carrier. Avoid posting full labels publicly, as they can reveal names and addresses. If the retailer supplied prepaid shipping, preserve that email too.

03Distinguish stages of the return

A carrier may show accepted, in transit, delivered to a facility, and received by retailer. A merchant may have a separate inspection and refund status. Record each stage without assuming a carrier-delivered scan means money has already been issued.

For delays, compare the timeline to the merchant’s documented processing estimates. Ask support whether the parcel is in intake, inspection or refund processing and request a case reference.

04Close the evidence file carefully

Save the refund confirmation and check that the credit appears in the relevant payment method. Processing can vary by merchant and payment provider. Do not discard return proof until the resolution is complete.

If a refund remains unresolved, send the seller a concise order-and-tracking timeline. Payment-provider dispute deadlines may be separate, so consult official terms promptly rather than waiting indefinitely.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A returned monitor is scanned as delivered by the carrier on Monday but remains “processing” at the merchant until Friday. The buyer contacts support with the authorization, shipping receipt and delivery scan instead of claiming the carrier lost the parcel.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Keep the authorization and carrier handoff receipt.
  2. Distinguish a generated label from accepted postage.
  3. Track retailer receipt and refund separately.
  4. Preserve records until the refund is confirmed.
