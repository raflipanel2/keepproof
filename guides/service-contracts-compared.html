FIELD GUIDE 08 / WARRANTIES

Extended service plans versus included warranties

A comparison method that separates overlapping promises from real benefits.

Published: 11 November 2026 · KeepProof Editorial · General education

An “extended warranty” offered at checkout may be a separately priced service contract rather than the product’s included written warranty. The value depends on what is covered, what overlaps, the provider’s obligations and the costs you still bear. A longer stated duration is not enough to justify a purchase.

01Start with coverage you already have

Read the included manufacturer warranty first. Record how long it lasts, which components are covered, available remedies, exclusions and shipping or service fees. Check whether a retailer provides additional return or protection terms.

Then place the paid plan alongside it in a two-column comparison. Highlight months where both cover the same failure. Those overlapping months may reduce the incremental value of the service plan.

02Look past the marketing title

Ask whether accidental damage, battery wear, power surges or routine maintenance are included or excluded. These are product- and contract-specific. Record deductibles, per-incident fees, claim caps, waiting periods, cancellation rights and authorized repair locations.

A plan described as “five years of protection” may begin on the original purchase date or after another warranty expires. The answer changes the actual additional duration you are buying.

03Compare total likely costs

Add upfront price, per-claim fees, potential shipping and time costs. Consider the likely repair price of the product and your ability to replace it. This is a budgeting exercise rather than a statistical prediction of failure.

Review who administers the contract and how disputes are handled. The FTC advises consumers to distinguish service contracts from included warranties; actual consumer protections depend on the agreement and location.

04Avoid unnecessary complexity

For inexpensive or easily replaced goods, self-funding a repair may be simpler. For specialized equipment, access to repair services and predictable costs may matter more. Neither choice is universally correct.

Store a copy of the plan and the original warranty in the same product folder. Label each document so you can quickly identify which organization to contact when a failure occurs.

IN PRACTICE / EXAMPLE

An ordinary example

A $240 kitchen appliance includes one year of manufacturer coverage. A $65 two-year service plan also starts on the purchase date and charges a $20 claim fee. The buyer compares how much coverage is genuinely additional before deciding whether the plan is worthwhile.

TAKE THIS WITH YOU

Action checklist

  1. Read the included warranty first.
  2. Map overlap month by month.
  3. List fees, exclusions and administrator details.
  4. Keep both contracts if you purchase the plan.
